How photolyase repairs DNA

Light-powered repair of UV damage, step by step

  • No cutting. Most DNA repair removes the damaged stretch and rebuilds it. Photolyase just reverses the chemistry, so it can't introduce copying errors.
  • The electron is lent, not spent. It returns to the flavin, so the enzyme ends exactly as it started and can repair the next dimer.
  • Who has it. Bacteria, plants, fungi and many animals (fish, frogs, marsupials). Placental mammals, including humans, lost it and use nucleotide excision repair instead.
